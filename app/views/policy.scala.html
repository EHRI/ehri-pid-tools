This page describes how and why EHRI assigns persistent identifiers (PIDs) to the resources it publishes, superseding the EHRI PID Roadmap, and should be considered the authoritative source for current EHRI PID policy.

This page is a work-in-progress and will be updated in a continuous, rolling manner.

Overview

EHRI uses different PID types depending on the type of resource being identified and who has custody and editorial control over the original data. The term "data", used here, may refer to several different types of resources, including (but not limited to): articles, websites, datasets, and metadata entries pertaining to both physical and intellectual entities.

Responsibilities and Curation

Responsibility for the accuracy and quality of PID metadata and the integrity of their relationship to the underlying resources rests with the EHRI-ERIC Central Hub.

Note: Scholarly outputs published in journals (and similar) are considered second order data produced by EHRI-ERIC (and its predecessor projects) and thus hosted by a third party, the HAL open science platform at https://hal.science/EHRI.

Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs)

EHRI assigns DOIs to resources for which we have editorial control and management responsibility for the publication lifecycle. These currently include posts on the EHRI Document Blog, datasets on the Geospatial Repository, and scholarly Online Editions. More resources may be added in future. EHRI uses DataCite to register DOIs with the prefix @config.doiPrefix.

DOI URLs typically resolve to a landing page which includes:

  • the complete DOI, in web-linked form;
  • a link to the underlying item (web page; dataset, etc);
  • citation metadata: title, DOI, author(s), publication date, rights/licence and version;
  • where applicable, a link to prior versions or related resources, translations, etc.

Versioning and Removal

A new DOI is minted only for a significant change to an item's content; minor corrections (spelling, grammar, formatting) retain the existing identifier. When a resource does receive a new DOI the old one will be linked as a "related identifier" where feasible.

If a resource is removed or becomes unavailable, its DOI landing page will remain accessible but return an HTTP 410 Gone status acknowledging the item's prior existence, rather than a 404 Not Found page. This is referred to as a "tombstone" page.

Quality Assurance

EHRI DOIs are subject to an automated quality assurance check with results available on the @Messages("pid.health.doi") page. This check takes the form of a periodic scan of DOIs and the entities (web page, website, dataset, etc.) to which they refer, recording any targets which are currently unavailable or not functioning correctly. Due to the nature of the web and potential for networking glitches, scheduled or unscheduled downtime, etc., the health check may report results which are sometimes inconsistent.

Responsibilities

Responsibilities between EHRI-ERIC and the DOI Service Provider will be governed by the DataCite Member Roles and Responsibilities.

Archival Resource Keys (ARKs)

Archival Resource Keys (ARKs) are used by EHRI to identify resources on the EHRI Portal such as collection-holding institutions, descriptions of archival material, authority records (people, corporate bodies, families), and controlled vocabularies. EHRI is registered with the ARK Alliance with Name Assigning Authority Number (NAAN) 41045 and "mints" its own ARKs, not relying on an external authority.

ARKs on the EHRI Portal share a common shoulder prefix "p0" to the section of the identifier following the NAAN. If resources external to the EHRI Portal are in future assigned ARKs with the same NAAN they will be given a distinct shoulder.

Unlike DOIs, ARK URLs resolve directly to the resource, not a landing page. EHRI ARKs support the "??" inflection; that is, appending "??" to the ARK URL will return a brief plain text metadata statement concerning the resource.

Versioning and Removal

ARK-identified resources on the EHRI Portal are not versioned, in that the content of resources they point to can (and do) change when updated by EHRI or the original data provider. The EHRI Portal provides information about when a resource was last updated.

When an ARK-identified resource is removed from the EHRI Portal the URL will show a tombstone page (with an HTTP 410 Gone status code) containing the date at which the item was deleted.

Quality Assurance

ARK PIDs on the EHRI Portal are subject to less stability than DOIs, simply because they often (but not always) refer to archival descriptions created by third party data providers and therefore subject to removal or reorganisation at the originator's discretion. While the EHRI Portal incorporates mechanisms to ensure that ARKs cannot be inadvertently (or purposefully) re-used or redirected in an inconsistent manner, PID consistency is ultimately only as stable as the aggregated data sources allow.

While not yet implemented we hope to provide more statistics on churn (removal and redirection) across ARK PIDs in future.

ORCID

ORCID identifiers are used to disambiguate researchers and record their contributions to EHRI outputs. Support currently differs by system: the EHRI Document Blog records contributor ORCID iDs as entered by administrators. Authenticated OAuth2 sign-in with ORCID on the EHRI Portal is planned, but not yet available.

Research Organization Registry (ROR)

EHRI is registered with the Research Organization Registry as ror:00zsk2t64, for use in affiliation and funder metadata, as well as the publisher of various resources identified by a DOI.

e-ISSN

The EHRI Document Blog, as a serial publication, has been assigned the electronic International Standard Serial Number (e-ISSN) 3118-0191.

Termination and/or Handover of PID services

Should EHRI-ERIC wind down and no longer be able to maintain PID metadata, responsibility for DOIs and other PIDs mediated by a third-party would be transferred to a suitable consortium member institution.
